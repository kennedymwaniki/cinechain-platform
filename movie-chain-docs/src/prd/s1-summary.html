1Executive Summary

CineChain is a multi-tenant SaaS platform that lets any cinema chain run its entire ticketing operation — venues, schedules, seat inventory, payments and admission — from one shared system, without ever seeing another chain's data.

The product in one page

Today, a cinema chain in this market has three options: an expensive per-site licensed system, a bespoke build, or a spreadsheet and a card machine. All three scale badly, and none give a chain executive a live view across their estate.

CineChain is sold as a subscription. A chain signs up, is onboarded in under a minute, and immediately begins configuring theatres, screens and seat layouts. They publish showtimes, sell tickets through a customer-facing web application, take payment by card or M-Pesa, and admit audiences by scanning a QR code. Their staff work in a console scoped to their chain; the platform operator works in a separate console that spans all chains.

Why this is built multi-tenant

The commercial case is the one set out in the companion masterclass: with separate installations, serving twice as many chains costs roughly twice as much. With multi-tenancy the marginal cost of a new chain approaches zero, which is what makes a subscription business viable at this price point.

The product case is less obvious and equally important. Because every chain runs the same version, a feature built for one is available to all, and a security fix reaches every customer the same afternoon. In a market where a single cinema chain would never fund the development of, say, dynamic pricing on its own, shared development is what makes the product good.

The architectural commitment this document makes

Tenancy is present from the first migration, not retrofitted. Every tenant-scoped table carries tenant_id; every such table has row-level securityPostgreSQL — Row Security Policies enabled; and the application connects as a role that cannot bypass it. A tenant's tier determines which database connection their requests receive — never which code runs.

This is a deliberate, front-loaded cost of roughly a week at the start of the project, taken in exchange for avoiding a re-platforming exercise later.
