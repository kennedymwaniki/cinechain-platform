5Functional Requirements

Requirements are numbered FR-n for reference in tickets and tests. Priorities follow MoSCoW: MUST ships in v1, SHOULD ships if the increment allows, COULD is explicitly deferred.

5.0 Customer identity — guest first

The primary booking path requires no account. This is not a concession to lazy users; it is how cinema ticketing actually works, and forcing registration in front of a ticket purchase is the single most reliable way to lose the sale.

IDRequirementPriNotes and constraints
FR-70A customer can complete an entire booking — browse, hold, pay, receive a ticket — without creating an account.MUSTName, e-mail and payment details are collected once, at payment. Nothing else.
FR-71A guest hold is owned by a signed, opaque hold_token issued by the server, not by a user identity.MUSTContains hold id and expiry; signed so it cannot be altered. Possession is the claim to the seat. See §7.3.
FR-72The ticket delivered by e-mail is a bearer credential: high-entropy, signed, single-use and not enumerable.MUSTWhoever holds it can use it — so it must not be guessable from a booking reference.
FR-73A guest can retrieve a booking via a signed, expiring link, or by booking reference plus the e-mail used.MUSTReference alone must never be sufficient — that is an enumeration oracle.
FR-74A customer may optionally register a CineChain account, which is platform-level and tied to no chain.SHOULDToken carries a consumer id and no tenant_id. See §6.2.
FR-75A registered customer can browse and book at any chain on the platform with one login.SHOULDThe travelling-customer case. A Nyati regular in Mombasa books at Prestige without a second account.
FR-76A registered customer sees their booking history across all chains they have used.SHOULDAssembled from their own bookings, scoped by consumer id — never by tenant. RLS holds one tenant per transaction, so a sanctioned SECURITY DEFINER lookup returns pointers (tenant, booking id) only; each chain's rows are then read in that chain's own scoped transaction.
FR-77When a guest later registers with an e-mail they have booked under, their prior bookings are linked to the new account.SHOULDKeyed on (tenant_id, email) on tenant_customer, but it does not happen by itself: a tenant-scoped job per chain sets consumer_id on matching guest rows. It runs only after the e-mail is verified, otherwise registering someone else's address would inherit their history.
FR-78Loyalty balances accrue per chain and are visible only to the chain that issued them.COULDHeld on tenant_customer, under RLS. One login, independent balances — the Uber/Bolt model.
FR-79No chain can read the platform consumer directory, or determine that a customer also uses another chain.MUSTEnforced structurally: platform.consumer lives outside the tenant boundary. consumer_id is never exposed in tenant-facing APIs.
The requirement that drives all of the above

Grace is a customer of the platform who transacts with chains. She is never a user of a chain. A design in which her identity carries a tenant_id satisfies FR-70 through FR-73 and then fails FR-75 outright — which is why §4.2 separates the three populations before any of this is implemented.

5.5 Seat selection and holds

This group is the heart of the system and is specified in full in Section 7. The requirements are stated here for completeness.

IDRequirementPriNotes and constraints
FR-40A customer can select one or more available seats and place a hold on them.MUSTMaximum 10 seats per hold, configurable per tenant.
FR-41A hold lasts 300 seconds and is configurable per tenant between 120 and 900 seconds.MUSTThe countdown shown to the customer derives from a server-supplied expires_at.
FR-42A held seat is unavailable to every other customer of that tenant for the duration of the hold.MUSTIncluding box-office terminals — one inventory, no exceptions.
FR-43A seat can be held by at most one party at any instant. Concurrent attempts must yield exactly one winner.MUSTEnforced by an atomic operation, not by read-then-write. See §7.2.
FR-44An expired hold releases its seats automatically, with no user action and no scheduled job on the critical path.MUSTRedis TTL is the mechanism; a sweeper reconciles the durable record.
FR-45A customer may extend a hold once, by 120 seconds, if no other customer is waiting on those seats.SHOULDReduces abandonment at the payment step without allowing indefinite squatting.
FR-46A customer may release a hold explicitly by leaving the seat-selection page.SHOULDReturns inventory sooner than TTL expiry.
FR-47Holds must not leave a single isolated empty seat between two bookings where avoidable.COULDSeat-gap prevention, driven by demand. Enforced only when projected occupancy reaches a per-tenant threshold (default 60 %) and the screening is more than 30 min away. A sell-out opening night protects every seat; a quiet showing a week later never blocks a customer's choice. Rejects only when a gap-free block of the same size and class exists, and offers it. Advisory, never a sale guarantee. Implementation plan step 13.
FR-48Box-office holds may be created with a shorter window and are attributed to the clerk.SHOULDDefault 90 seconds — the customer is standing there.

The seat-selection screen

Four details on this screen carry disproportionate weight, and each has a requirement behind it:

  • The countdown is rendered from the server's expires_at, never from a client-side timer started on page load. A client timer drifts, survives a laptop sleeping, and will eventually tell a customer they have two minutes left when the seats went thirty seconds ago.
  • Taken seats are greyed, never hidden. People navigate a seat map by its shape. Removing seats changes the geometry and makes the auditorium unrecognisable.
  • Held and sold look identical. Distinguishing them would leak live demand data to anyone who opens the page, including competitors.
  • The map updates without a refresh. Nothing erodes trust faster than clicking a seat that was sold twenty seconds ago and being told it is unavailable.

5.6 Payment and ticketing

IDRequirementPriNotes and constraints
FR-50A customer can pay for a held booking by card or M-Pesa.MUSTProvider-agnostic interface; at least one of each in v1.
FR-51Payment initiation is idempotent, keyed on the hold identifier.MUSTA retried request must never charge twice.
FR-52Seats convert from HELD to SOLD only after payment is confirmed, in a single database transaction with the booking.MUSTNo partially-confirmed bookings may exist at any point.
FR-53If payment succeeds but the hold has expired and the seats are gone, the payment is automatically refunded and the customer told clearly.MUSTThe most important failure path in the system. See §7.4.
FR-54A confirmed booking issues one ticket per seat, each with a unique scannable code.MUSTCodes are signed and single-use; not guessable by enumeration.
FR-55Tickets are delivered by SMS and e-mail within 30 seconds, and are always retrievable in the booking view.MUSTDelivery failure must not fail the booking.
FR-56Payment provider webhooks are verified, idempotent and tenant-attributed.MUSTWebhooks arrive with no user session; tenant context comes from the stored payment record.
FR-57A clerk can take cash and record the tender type.MUSTSame booking flow, different settlement: a cash provider settles immediately, payment.tender_type is recorded with the clerk's id.
