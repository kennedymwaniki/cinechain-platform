The design load is deliberately skewed. A realistic peak is not 800 evenly distributed requests per second; it is 760 requests per second from Nyati and 40 from everyone else combined. Capacity planning and load testing must both reflect that shape, because a system tuned for an even distribution fails in exactly the scenario it will meet.

The support-access problem, stated honestly

Sooner or later a tenant will report a bug that cannot be diagnosed without looking at their data. Because operators deliberately have no such access, a mechanism is required — and if one is not designed, someone will eventually be granted a production database password, which is the worst possible version of it.

The designed mechanism: a tenant admin grants time-boxed support access from their own console. It expires automatically, defaults to two hours, is limited to named support staff, records every query executed under it, and is visible to the tenant afterwards. Access is never granted by the platform to itself.
