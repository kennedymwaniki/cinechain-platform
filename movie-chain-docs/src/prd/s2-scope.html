2Problem, Goals and Scope

The problem

A cinema chain operating across several towns faces a set of problems that compound with every additional site.

Inventory is fragmented
Each theatre knows its own seats. Nobody has a live picture of the estate, so a chain cannot tell which screenings are underselling while there is still time to act.
Scheduling is manual and error-prone
A blockbuster shows four times a day in one town and twice in another, and the schedule is built in a spreadsheet. Double-booking a screen is a regular occurrence and is discovered by an audience.
Online selling is bolted on
Where online sales exist, they usually run against a separate inventory from the box office, which produces the worst possible failure: a seat sold twice, discovered at the door.
There is no reliable seat-hold mechanism
Customers abandon a booking half-way and the seat stays blocked, or the seat is never blocked at all and two customers pay for it.

Goals

  1. One live seat inventory shared by the website, the mobile app and the box-office terminal, with a guarantee that a seat can be sold exactly once.
  2. A five-minute hold that gives a customer time to pay without allowing abandoned baskets to starve inventory.
  3. Chain-wide visibility — occupancy, revenue and performance across every theatre in one view.
  4. Self-service onboarding so that the platform's growth is not limited by engineering time.
  5. Verifiable tenant isolation, enforced by the database rather than by developer discipline.
  6. Four commercial tiers served by one codebase, differing only in configuration.

Non-goals for version 1

Stating these explicitly prevents the scope creep that kills first releases.

  • Food and beverage sales — a substantial product in its own right, deferred.
  • Loyalty and membership schemes — planned for a later increment; the data model leaves room.
  • Dynamic and surge pricing — v1 supports fixed pricing per seat class and per screening.
  • Distributor settlement and film-hire reporting — exports are provided; reconciliation is not.
  • Native mobile applications — a responsive web app first.
  • Multi-currency — KES only in v1, though amounts are stored with an explicit currency from the start.
  • Unreserved (free) seating — every screening in v1 is allocated seating.
A deliberate exception

Multi-currency is a non-goal but the schema carries a currency column from day one, and money is stored in minor units as integers. Retrofitting currency into a money model is disproportionately painful relative to carrying the column from the start. This is the one place where we pay a small cost now against a likely future requirement — and it is worth naming the reasoning, because doing this everywhere is how a v1 never ships.
